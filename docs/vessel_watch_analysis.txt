This briefing on Indian energy imports as of June 7, 2026, presents a highly unusual and likely incomplete picture due to significantly stale AIS data, nearly four days old. The most striking observation is the complete absence of any detected vessels inbound to India or within the critical Hormuz pipeline, making any real-time assessment unreliable. This severe data lag renders projections difficult and suggests substantial gaps in current visibility.

Currently, eight energy vessels are identified at Indian ports: seven crude tankers and one LNG carrier. Crude imports are concentrated at Paradip (four vessels), Mundra (two vessels), and Kochi (one vessel), underscoring these as key refining and storage hubs across both the east and west coasts. Paradip, in particular, shows strong crude activity with all four vessels berthed. Kochi is the sole port in this snapshot handling LNG, with one vessel anchored alongside a crude tanker, indicating its dual-commodity role for the west coast.

A critical data gap exists concerning the Hormuz pipeline and overall inbound traffic. No vessels with confirmed India destinations were detected in or near the Gulf, nor were any observed transiting Hormuz or the Gulf of Oman without a declared destination. Furthermore, there are zero vessels of any cargo type identified as currently inbound to India. This complete lack of detected pre-arrival activity is highly atypical for India's energy landscape and is almost certainly a direct consequence of the aged AIS data rather than an accurate reflection of the supply chain.

The snapshot highlights a significant imbalance with crude oil dominating the vessels present at ports (seven crude to one LNG). The complete absence of CNG vessels is also noted. However, without any current inbound traffic, it's impossible to determine any real shift from typical Indian import patterns. Given the stale AIS data and the complete lack of identified inbound or Gulf-region vessels, no reliable short-term outlook for arrivals in the next 24-48 hours can be provided. The current data offers a limited and backward-looking view, insufficient for dynamic supply chain analysis or identifying any immediate risks or patterns.
