India's live crude oil imports for the week ending June 7, 2026, totaled 525,000 metric tons, equivalent to approximately 3,848,250 barrels. This translates to an estimated daily crude intake of around 549,750 barrels per day. When viewed against India's substantial refining capacity of approximately 5.5 million barrels per day, this weekly figure represents about 10% of the nation's total processing capability, highlighting the continuous and significant throughput required by the refining sector.

Comparing the available data, the live scraper figure for weekly crude imports (525,000 MT) is a fraction of the official April 2026 data, which reported a weekly average of 4,640,436 MT for POL (Petroleum, Oil, Lubricants) & Crude Products. This notable disparity arises because the official data encompasses a broader category of petroleum products alongside crude, reflecting total port movements for the sector, whereas the live scraper data specifically tracks crude oil tonnage from vessel arrivals. The official figure also pertains to the previous month, April, while the live data covers the first week of June.

It is crucial to acknowledge several caveats regarding the data completeness. The live scraper data is derived from only three unique vessels observed over the past seven days, arriving at specific major ports, primarily Paradip (425,324 MT) and Deendayal (99,676 MT). While the data quality is assessed as 'adequate,' this method may not capture all crude movements across every Indian port, nor does it account for vessels whose tonnage data is not yet reported or processed. The official data, conversely, aggregates cargo across all major ports, providing a more comprehensive, albeit less granular, view of the broader POL and crude segment.

Looking ahead, India's crude import demand is expected to remain robust. With a vast refining capacity and strong domestic energy consumption driven by economic growth, the country is a key player in global crude markets. The consistent need to feed its refineries suggests that crude oil imports will maintain their high levels, making granular, real-time data increasingly valuable for tracking supply chain dynamics and market shifts, despite the inherent limitations in capturing every single movement.
